Fractals: infinite detail

Your rule says: replace every straight line with a tiny copy of your drawing. Do that again and again, forever, and you get a fractal. Zoom in on any piece and it looks like the whole thing.

Infinite length in a finite space

The Koch rule turns 1 line into 4 lines, each ⅓ as long. So every level the length grows by ×4⁄3. After 100 levels it's over 3 trillion times longer, yet the snowflake still fits on your screen. Its area stays finite (exactly 8⁄5 of the starting triangle).

Dimension 1.26?!

A line is 1-dimensional and a square is 2-dimensional. Fractals sit in between. If your rule makes N copies, each scaled by r, the fractal dimension is

D = log N ⁄ log(1/r)

Koch: log 4 ⁄ log 3 ≈ 1.2619. With pieces of different sizes we solve r₁ᴰ + r₂ᴰ + … = 1 instead. That's what the counter on screen does.

How long is the coastline of Britain? It depends on your ruler: the smaller the ruler, the longer the coast. Real coastlines behave like fractals, with dimension about 1.25.
