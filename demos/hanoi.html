Recursion: the secret move

To move a tower of n disks:

  1. Move the top n−1 disks out of the way (that's a smaller version of the same puzzle!).
  2. Move the biggest disk.
  3. Move the n−1 disks back on top of it.

A problem that solves itself using smaller copies of itself is called recursive.

Counting the moves

If M(n) is the fewest moves for n disks, then M(n) = 2·M(n−1) + 1, which works out to M(n) = 2ⁿ − 1.

1, 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, 127, 255… Each extra disk doubles the work. Mathematicians prove formulas like this with mathematical induction.

Legend says monks are moving 64 golden disks, one per second. They'd need 2⁶⁴ − 1 ≈ 18 quintillion moves, which is about 585 billion years. The universe is only about 14 billion years old.

Where it shows up

  • Computer science: recursive algorithms and stacks
  • Binary numbers: disk k moves every 2k steps
  • Gray codes and the Sierpinski triangle (yes, really!)
