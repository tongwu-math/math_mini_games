Symmetry is a group

A symmetry is a move that leaves a picture looking exactly the same. Your kaleidoscope has two kinds:

  • Rotations: turn by 360°/n, or 2 of those, or 3…
  • Reflections (with Mirror on): flip across one of the blue lines

Moves you can combine

Do one symmetry move, then another, and you get a third symmetry move. Every move can be undone. A collection like that is called a group, and studying them is group theory.

With just rotations you get the cyclic group Cn with n moves. Add mirrors and you get the dihedral group Dn with 2n moves: n rotations plus n flips. Every line you draw gets copied once per move.

Symmetry in nature

  • Snowflakes: D6, thanks to how water molecules link up
  • Starfish: D5 · many flowers: D5 or D6
  • Crystals, tiles, and wallpaper patterns
Any flat pattern that repeats forever in two directions, from bathroom tiles to M.C. Escher art, uses one of exactly 17 symmetry groups. Mathematicians proved there can never be an 18th.

Where it shows up

  • Chemistry: molecule shapes predict how they react
  • Physics: symmetries lead to conservation laws
  • Solving the Rubik's Cube is group theory in your hands
