Size

Light switches are secretly algebra

Call a light that's on a 1 and a light that's off a 0. Pressing a button adds 1 to five lights, using the rule 1 + 1 = 0. That's arithmetic mod 2, the math of "even or odd."

Two big shortcuts

  • Order doesn't matter. Press A then B, or B then A: same board. Addition is commutative.
  • Pressing twice cancels out. So a solution never needs to press any button more than once.

That means a solution is just a list of buttons to press once each. With 25 buttons, that's 25 unknowns, each 0 or 1.

Solving it like a math class problem

Each light gives one equation: "the presses on me and my neighbors must add up to my starting value (mod 2)." That's a system of linear equations, 25 equations and 25 unknowns, and the Hint button solves it with Gaussian elimination, the same method you use for two equations in two unknowns, just bigger.

On the 5×5 board, only 1 out of every 4 random patterns of lights can be solved at all! Linear algebra proves it: two of the equations are "redundant," which cuts the reachable boards down by a factor of 2 × 2 = 4. (This game only gives you the solvable ones.)

Where it shows up

  • Error-correcting codes that keep QR codes and space-probe messages readable
  • Computer graphics, search engines and AI all run on linear algebra
  • Cryptography and computer chips use math mod 2 constantly
